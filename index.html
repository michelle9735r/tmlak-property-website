Project Overview

Burj Binghatti Jacob & Co Residences is a supertall tower under construction in Business Bay, Dubai, developed by Binghatti Properties in partnership with luxury jewelry and watch house Jacob & Co. Announced in November 2022, it's designed around Jacob & Co's horology and jewelry design language — the tower's crown is modeled on the brand's signature diamond cut. Sales officially launched in June 2023, with above-ground construction beginning in February 2025.

The building is planned at approximately 557 metres across roughly 104–105 floors, positioning it among the tallest residential towers under construction globally.

Project Overview

The Oasis is a large-scale, low-density villa and mansion community by Emaar Properties in Dubailand — Emaar's first "blue lagoon" residential development, built around waterways, lagoons and canals rather than towers. It was launched on 13 June 2023 at a gala event at the Armani Hotel, Burj Khalifa. The masterplan spans roughly 100 million sq ft (about 9.4 million sqm) and is planned to eventually include over 7,000 residences, primarily large villas and mansions, released across multiple phases rather than a single launch.

As of early 2026, released phases reported by third-party sources include Lavita, Palmiera, Palmiera 2, Palmiera 3, Mirage, and more recently Palmiera Collective and Mareva 2 (January 2026) — with further phased launches expected to continue.

DAMAC Islands 2 is the second phase of DAMAC's island-themed master community in Dubailand, officially launched in November 2025 following the sell-out success of the original DAMAC Islands. Core investment concepts,

01

Gross vs. net yield

Gross yield is annual rent divided by purchase price. Net yield subtracts service charges, fees and vacancy — the number that actually reflects your return.

02

Off-plan vs. ready

Off-plan trades construction-period risk for lower entry pricing and staged payments. Ready property trades a pricing premium for immediate income and certainty.

03

Community selection

Infrastructure trajectory, supply pipeline and tenant demand shape a community's medium-term performance more than any single unit's finishes.

04

Developer analysis

Delivery history and build quality — not marketing renders — are what determine whether a handover date and a finish level hold up.

05

Payment-plan analysis

A "low" down payment can still mean high total exposure if post-handover installments are steep — model the full schedule, not just the entry number.

06

Capital appreciation

Driven by supply-demand balance and infrastructure investment in a district — not guaranteed by any single project's marketing.

07

Rental strategy

Short-term holiday-let income differs meaningfully from long-term lease income in yield, effort and regulatory requirements.

08

Investment risk

Construction delay, market cycle timing and currency exposure are the three risk categories every off-plan investor should weigh explicitly.

The Yield Trap: Why smart Dubai investors look beyond gross ROI.

Why the number on the brochure and the number in your account are rarely the same — and how to close that gap before you buy, not after.

A headline yield is the easiest number in real estate to make attractive, and the easiest to misread.

When a listing advertises an 8% or 9% yield, that figure is almost always gross — annual rent divided by purchase price, nothing subtracted. It's a real number, but it's not the number that lands in your account. Between the headline and your actual return sits service charges, vacancy periods, management fees, and — for off-plan purchases — the opportunity cost of capital tied up before the unit ever produces income.

Where the gap actually comes from

Three things consistently separate gross yield from what an investor keeps: annual service charges (which scale with unit size and building amenities, not with rent), periods of vacancy between tenants, and — for shorter-term or holiday-let strategies — the cost of active management. None of these are hidden exactly, but they're rarely printed next to the headline number, so the burden falls on the buyer to ask.

Why off-plan yields need extra scrutiny

An off-plan project quoting a yield before it has a single paying tenant is quoting a projection, not a track record. That's not disqualifying — projections are a normal part of evaluating a launch — but it changes what questions matter: what comparable, completed projects in the same community are actually renting for today, and how the developer's past handovers have tracked against their own original timelines.

Three questions worth asking before you trust a yield figure

1. Is this gross or net? If a source doesn't specify, assume gross, and ask for the net calculation directly.

2. What's actually renting nearby, today? Comparable, currently-tenanted units in the same community are a better anchor than a projection.

3. What does the payment schedule do to your effective entry price? A steep post-handover payment plan changes your real capital-at-risk timeline, which changes your real return.

The takeaway

A high yield isn't a reason to buy on its own, and a modest one isn't automatically a reason to pass. The number worth trusting is the one you've stress-tested against real comparables and real costs — which is a conversation, not a single figure on a listing page.

Home / Insights / Golden Visa

The UAE Golden Visa, through a Dubai property purchase.

A federal residency program, not a TMLAK product — here's how the property route actually works, and what's worth confirming before you count on it.

The Golden Visa is a UAE federal program — it isn't specific to Dubai, and it isn't something TMLAK administers. What we can do is help you buy a property that meets the investment criteria, and point you toward the right official channels for the visa application itself.

How the property route works, broadly

The UAE's 10-year renewable Golden Visa can be obtained through real estate investment, with a minimum property value threshold that has been publicly set at AED 2 million. Ownership can be individual or, in some cases, through specific financing structures — the exact eligibility rules and required documentation are set and updated by UAE federal immigration authorities, not by any brokerage.

What we'd help with — and what we wouldn't claim to

What we can help with: identifying Dubai properties that meet the published investment threshold, structuring the purchase, and connecting you with the right legal/immigration specialists to handle the visa application itself.

What we wouldn't claim: a guarantee of visa approval, a specific processing timeline, or that TMLAK handles the immigration filing directly. Visa eligibility, processing and approval sit with UAE federal authorities — always confirm current requirements with an immigration lawyer or the official UAE government channels before treating any property purchase as visa-guaranteed.

Questions worth asking before you buy with this in mind

1. Does the property, structure and price actually meet the current published threshold? Thresholds and rules can be updated — confirm the current figure with an immigration specialist, not a listing page.

2. Is the ownership structure eligible? Off-plan, mortgaged, or jointly-owned property can carry different eligibility conditions.

3. Who is actually handling the visa filing? A property purchase and a visa application are two separate processes handled by different parties — know who's doing which before you assume it's covered.

The takeaway

The property side of this is where TMLAK can genuinely help — finding the right asset at the right structure. The visa side deserves its own dedicated, current-as-of-today advice from an immigration specialist, not a paragraph on a real estate website.
